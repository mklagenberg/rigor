# Source discovery

Source discovery is a deliberate stage, not an incidental search result list.

## Discovery map

For each decision-critical claim, map possible evidence classes:

- primary records, datasets, filings, laws, standards, contracts, and direct measurements;
- accountable institutions, regulators, maintainers, and authors;
- domain experts with relevant, demonstrable competence;
- affected communities and practitioners with first-hand experience;
- independent journalists, auditors, researchers, and critics;
- ownership, funding, procurement, lobbying, sponsorship, and market relationships.

## Authority and expert assessment

Authority is claim-specific. Evaluate whether a person or institution has direct access, relevant competence, disclosed method, a track record that can be inspected, and material incentives. Do not infer reliability solely from title, employer, follower count, official role, or credentials outside the claim’s domain.

For community evidence, record identity confidence, scope, selection mechanism, reproducibility, and whether a recurring pattern can be independently checked.

## Discovery stopping rule

Stop adding sources when new sources no longer add an independent lineage, a material counterclaim, a needed population, or a better primary record. More citations with the same origin are noise, not rigor.
