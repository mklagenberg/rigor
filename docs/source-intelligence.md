# Source intelligence

RIGOR learns about sources through contextual profiles, not a universal trust score.

A profile MAY record publication context, intended audience, disclosure obligations, relevant competence, evidence access, incentives, observed patterns that are supported by inspectable cases, and revalidation date. For example, a company filing directed to investors and a marketing page from the same company have different obligations and different risks of framing.

Profiles MUST preserve the distinction between source context and the truth of a specific claim. They MUST NOT use confidential material, turn unverified allegations into labels, or imply hidden interests without evidence. Promotion of a durable profile rule requires human review and a recorded basis.

Every live investigation still assesses the particular source, claim, date, method, and lineage; a profile is a prompt for scrutiny, never a shortcut around verification.
