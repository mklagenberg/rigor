# Hypotheses and adaptive research loops

Use a hypothesis when an explanation, forecast, causal account, or counterfactual guides what to inspect next. It is a working object, not a claim or evidence.

## Register

Each hypothesis has an ID, type (`retrospective`, `prospective`, `causal`, or `counterfactual`), statement, alternatives, expected traces, disconfirming conditions, linked claims, confidence rationale, and status. A hypothesis may guide a search or test; it cannot repair a missing record or raise a claim's support state.

Treat a missing record carefully. Before it weakens a hypothesis, assess whether the relevant archive or population was covered, whether the record could have survived, whether access was possible, and whether search terms and identity resolution were adequate.

## Frontier and loop

After every bounded pass, translate material next steps into frontier tasks. A task must state its linked hypothesis or question, expected information gain, effort, permission boundary, priority, status, and rationale. Prioritize discriminating tests over more examples of the leading interpretation.

Default iteration limits include the initial pass: Level 3 = 2, Level 4 = 3, Level 5 = 4. A user may set another finite limit. Continue automatically only inside the approved scope, budget, permissions, and limit. Re-approval is mandatory for material expansion, private/paid/sensitive access, contact, or another external effect.

End with one explicit state: `exhausted-within-scope`, `blocked`, `paused-by-limit`, `ready-for-revalidation`, or `escalated`. This is distinct from revalidation, which follows an accepted conclusion when an external trigger occurs.
