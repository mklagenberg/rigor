# 0008 — Hypothesis-driven adaptive research exhaustion

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-08-14
- Owner: Mauricio M. Klagenberg
- Change Set: 2026-08-14-hypothesis-and-adaptive-exhaustion

## Decision

RIGOR adopts explicit hypothesis registers and an adaptive research-frontier loop for incomplete pasts, uncertain presents, and possible futures. Prospecting/futures work is a specialization of this general hypothesis mechanism, not a separate methodology.

Recommendations and next-record plans generated by an iteration become frontier candidates for the next iteration. The orchestrator executes only material, prioritized candidates within a finite approved loop policy. It records whether the investigation is exhausted within scope, blocked, paused by limit, ready for revalidation, or escalated.

## Rationale

A final section labelled “next steps” is not operational state. It leaves valuable hypotheses and decisive-record searches disconnected from the investigation that created them. Conversely, unbounded autonomous looping can consume resources, repeat correlated searches, and turn lack of evidence into manufactured certainty.

## Consequences

- Hypotheses remain distinct from claims and facts.
- Search absence is interpreted only after coverage assessment.
- Priority follows discriminating value and decision impact, not curiosity or link count.
- The Research Bundle preserves hypotheses, frontier tasks, iteration history, and loop outcome.
- Loop limits can be overridden at intake, while material expansion remains subject to re-approval.
- Revalidation remains a post-acceptance maintenance activity, distinct from an open-investigation loop.
