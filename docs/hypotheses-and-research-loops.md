# Hypotheses and adaptive research loops

## Purpose

RIGOR uses explicit hypotheses to investigate incomplete pasts, uncertain presents, and possible futures. A hypothesis is a testable working explanation, not an accepted fact and not permission to fill an evidentiary gap.

The same mechanism has three applications:

- **retrospective / abductive:** which explanation best accounts for incomplete historical traces?
- **prospective:** under stated conditions, what outcome is more likely in a stated horizon?
- **counterfactual:** what traces, differences, or consequences would be expected if a hypothesis were true or false?

## Hypothesis contract

Every material working hypothesis MUST record its statement, type, alternatives, supporting and conflicting claims, expected traces, disconfirming evidence, confidence rationale, and status.

Allowed statuses are `open`, `strengthened`, `weakened`, `supported`, `contested`, `rejected`, and `blocked`.

A hypothesis MAY generate searches, calculations, scenarios, and discriminating tests. It MUST NOT become a fact, close a relationship, assign an identity, or fill a missing datum unless independently supported by the evidence ledger.

Absence of an expected record weakens a hypothesis only after assessing whether the relevant record should exist, survived, is accessible, and was searched with adequate identity, language, spelling, date, and jurisdiction coverage.

## Research frontier

The **research frontier** is the bounded, ordered set of unresolved tasks that could materially change a hypothesis, conclusion, or decision. Each task links to a hypothesis or open question and records evidence target, expected information gain, priority, status, feasibility, and the iteration that created it.

Prioritize tasks by:

1. discriminating value between credible alternatives;
2. impact on the central conclusion or decision;
3. expected source quality, independence, and accessibility;
4. feasibility within the approved permissions, time, and cost; and
5. risk of redundant or correlated evidence.

An interesting task is not automatically a priority task. A record that distinguishes competing identities outranks a repeated or merely decorative occurrence of the same surname.

## Adaptive exhaustion loop

RIGOR treats recommendations generated by a completed pass as input to the next pass while the investigation remains open.

1. Execute the current bounded workstream set.
2. Update sources, claims, hypotheses, limitations, and handoffs.
3. Extract new candidate tasks from unresolved hypotheses, contradictions, missing decisive evidence, and challenger findings.
4. Score and order the frontier; close, defer, or block non-actionable tasks with reasons.
5. Execute the highest-value tasks within the approved loop limit.
6. Preserve an iteration journal and repeat until a stop condition is reached.

The loop is adaptive, not an unattended retry. It MUST NOT repeat a search merely because an iteration remains available. It MUST stop early when the remaining frontier has no material discriminating task.

## Defaults, overrides, and approval

Default maximum iterations, including the initial investigation pass, are:

| Level | Default maximum |
|---|---:|
| 3 | 2 |
| 4 | 3 |
| 5 | 4 |

The brief MAY override this with a numeric maximum or an explicit bounded policy such as `until-diminishing-returns`. The policy still requires finite time/cost and permission boundaries.

The orchestrator may create the next iteration without asking again when it remains within the approved scope, permissions, loop limit, and budget. It MUST request re-approval for a material scope or cost expansion, new sensitive data, paid/private access, contact with a person or institution, or any external action.

## Completion states

The dossier records one of these frontier outcomes:

- `exhausted-within-scope`: no remaining task has material expected information gain within mandate;
- `blocked`: decisive evidence is inaccessible or requires permission not granted;
- `paused-by-limit`: the configured loop/time/cost limit was reached with actionable tasks remaining;
- `ready-for-revalidation`: the investigation is accepted and only future change triggers remain;
- `escalated`: human direction is required before continuing.

`exhausted-within-scope` never means that all possible evidence has been found or that the conclusion is certain.

## Iteration journal

Each iteration records its objective, executed task IDs, new source IDs, changed hypothesis IDs, closed task IDs, outcome, and frontier decision. A dossier shows the current frontier and the journal so that a future continuation begins from the real research state rather than a prose summary.
