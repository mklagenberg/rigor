# Research process

## 1. Frame

Write the decision or learning objective, question, scope, exclusions, stakes, constraints, and activation level. Split ambiguous questions into answerable claims.

**Gate:** no collection before a Level 3–5 rationale and a bounded scope exist.

## 2. Map

Create a claim map: entities, timelines, stakeholders, likely evidence, authorities, communities, specialists, incentives, data sources, and unknowns. Mark which claims are decision-critical.

## 3. Plan

Create independent workstreams by evidence lineage or question type, not simply by search query. Define source-discovery routes, expected outputs, stop conditions, tool permissions, and a challenge workstream for Level 4–5.

## 4. Collect

Gather sources, preserve URLs/identifiers and dates, capture primary records where available, and log access limits. Collection must not silently convert a source claim into fact.

## 5. Verify

Assess source provenance and claim fitness. Normalize definitions and units. Group correlated sources. Identify missing primary evidence and contradictions.

## 6. Analyze

Link claims to evidence. Reproduce material calculations. Compare claims under shared definitions. State assumptions for every inference.

## 7. Challenge

Attempt to disprove the leading interpretation. Search for contrary primary evidence, alternative causal explanations, selection effects, survivorship bias, missing denominators, temporal mismatch, and incentives that could produce the observed narrative.

## 8. Synthesize

Answer the original question, not merely the collected facts. State conclusion, confidence, what is known, what is inferred, counterevidence, limitations, and what would change the answer.

## 9. Approve

For Level 4–5 material decisions, the named human decides whether evidence is sufficient and accepts residual risk. The approval does not convert uncertainty into fact.

## 10. Revalidate

Record events, dates, and source changes that trigger a refresh. Assign a revalidation owner for Level 4–5 investigations.
