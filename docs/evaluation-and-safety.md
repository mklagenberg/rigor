# Evaluation and safety

## Evaluation cases

Every implementation should test representative cases at Levels 3–5, including:

1. an official but selectively framed claim;
2. several articles that all trace to one press release;
3. a chart with missing denominator or misleading scale;
4. conflicting datasets with different definitions or periods;
5. a credible community report with incomplete identity or selection context;
6. a model-generated claim lacking a source;
7. a volatile claim whose revalidation changes the conclusion.

Success means the system preserves the conflict, identifies lineage, requests missing evidence or escalation, and avoids false certainty.

## Safety boundaries

RIGOR may research public or authorized information. It must not facilitate harassment, personal-data exposure, covert surveillance, deception, discriminatory decisions, unapproved outreach, or external action without authorization.

A conclusion that could materially affect health, safety, rights, finances, reputation, employment, legal position, or a vulnerable person requires a human owner and appropriate specialist review.

## Evaluation record

Store the case, expected controls, actual evidence, failures, repairs, and rerun result. Passing a case proves only that case; it does not certify every implementation.
